The Bullwhip Effect

TinyCare Baby Products — The Amplification Trap
Baby diaper consumption is rock-steady. Yet TinyCare's supply chain lurches between idle lines and mandatory overtime. Your job: diagnose why stable demand produces chaos, and fix it — one decision at a time.

📚 The Scenario

"Meera, I have the plant manager telling me he needs a third shift, the fluff pulp supplier threatening to derate us, and a warehouse holding fourteen weeks of finished goods. Household consumption grew 3% last year — steadily. Somebody explain how stable consumption produces this chaos."
— Rajiv Malhotra, MD, TinyCare Baby Products

You play as Meera Joshi, newly appointed supply chain analyst at TinyCare. Over 10 decision points, you'll uncover why the bullwhip effect turns stable demand into supply chain chaos — and what to do about it.

🏠 The Four-Echelon Supply Chain

TinyCare's supply chain has four links. Only the Retailer sees actual consumer demand. Every other tier forecasts from a signal that has already been distorted.

Demand: Normal(60, 10) — mean 60 cases/period, SD of 10. The ordering rule: Order = 2 × Incoming Order − Ending Inventory. Each tier doubles what it received and subtracts stock on hand.

🎯 Learning Objectives

1. Measure variance amplification using the Bullwhip Ratio = SD(Orders) / SD(Demand)

2. Identify the five root causes: demand signal processing, order batching, price fluctuation, shortage gaming, and lead time

3. Compare forecasting methods and their impact on upstream variability

4. Compute the cost of the bullwhip in inventory, stockouts and capacity

5. Design a coordination mechanism (POS sharing, VMI, CPFR) to dampen amplification

🏆 Scoring

30% Bullwhip Ratio Reduction — cumulative amplification vs the 41.3× naive baseline

25% Total Supply Chain Cost — holding + stockout + capacity across all tiers

20% Root Cause Diagnosis — correctly identifying which of Krajewski's five causes is active

15% Forecasting Method Selection — appropriate method and parameter choice

10% Coordination Mechanism Design — quality of the Round 7 recommendation
